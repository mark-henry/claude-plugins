## Evidence notes, corrections and open questions

Model-behaviour evidence ages; every figure above carries its model or year where known. Corrections on record, where the later correction wins and both are kept: the debate-versus-majority figures were once written in swapped order (correct: on GSM8K with gpt-3.5-turbo-0301 (2023), at nine responses per method (debate: three agents, two rounds), majority vote or self-consistency scored 88.2 percent, debate 83.0 percent and one answer 76.7 percent, the authors attributing the gain to consistency across generations, not self-correction [5]); the auditor figures were once given as 53.7 and 81.5 percent and placed in the wrong venue (correct: 55 and 82 percent; arXiv 2509.08713, 2025, NeurIPS 2025 AI4Science workshop [24]); the forking-paths figure was once given as "86 percent of flawed reports" (correct: 86 percent, 137 of 160, of persona-primed analyses passed AI review [26]); a session-ceiling figure was misattributed to one vendor; earlier notes wrongly attributed a leaderboard takedown to opposition to the metric (the reported trigger was a leak), attributed supervisors, caps and stall stops to the original bare while-loop (those were later additions), and called a reset plus force-push a revert; the sample bound in Cost is theoretical and assumes a perfect verifier; the "15 times" multi-agent figure is relative to chat, not to a single agent [2]; the 103-word prompt story is one team's anecdote from a hackathon weekend (Claude Code with Sonnet, August 2025) [30]; an earlier claim that mixing models itself caused debate damage was wrong: in "Talk Isn't Always Cheap" weaker agents persuaded stronger ones away from correct answers while the fully mixed group gained (source identity and conditions not fully recorded; unverified), so infer no general anti-mixing rule from it.

More on debate and voting: over seven benchmarks with five agents, majority voting averaged 0.769 against 0.738 for the best debate variant on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and 0.724 against 0.699 on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, while using fewer calls; the authors read this as parity in most cases (NeurIPS 2025) [6]. Voting beat consensus by up to 13.2 percent on reasoning tasks and consensus beat voting by up to 2.8 percent on knowledge tasks; more agents helped slightly while more discussion rounds before voting slightly reduced accuracy (Llama 3 8B and 70B; the rounds and agents test on StrategyQA only; there the vote follows three rounds of discussion, a decision protocol inside debate, not a vote over independent samples) [7]. This evidence is on 2023-2024 models (GPT-3.5 [5]; Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B, with Qwen2.5-32B and a GPT-4 check [6]; Llama 3 8B and 70B [7]); date it, and do not write it as a ban on all multi-agent talk: a relay of fresh challengers is not debate. A distinct setting is scalable oversight: debate may help when the judge is weaker than the debaters or lacks their information (Khan et al., ICML 2024; read secondhand, unverified) [49]; that is a ruling between opposing cases, not evidence for agent-only consensus.

Background, not restated here: for published long-run facts, consult the dated primary engineering reports in [15], [27] and [37] afresh before sizing how long a worker's task may run. Source pointers not yet verified (missing coverage, not completed pointers): the February 2026 agent-autonomy study of turn durations [53]; the task-time-horizon paper (March 2025) and its January 2026 update, whose page calls measurements above 16 hours unreliable with its current task suite [54]; and a May 2026 large-fleet spend account, which reported spend but no accepted-result or quality denominator, so its spend is no evidence of payoff [55]. Before using any of them to size a run or support a payoff claim, open and read the primary, record its verified title, date and URL in Sources and cite that entry at the sizing or payoff rule; until then the claim is unavailable. The public packs, tools and writers surveyed for this skill (superpowers [31], gstack [32], compound-engineering [33], agent-skills, spec-kit, council and jury tools, Ralph-style loops, vendor engineering posts [1][2][3]) are dated snapshots as of 2026-11; re-check their maturity before relying on them.

Open questions the evidence does not cover: no controlled study of throughput against agent count; no RCT of heavy multi-agent workflows; no rigorous controlled comparison of fresh-context Ralph-style loops against interactive use on the same task, and no aggregate success or failure rate for such loops (showcase reports are not that comparison); no measured discard rate or reviewer ceiling; little on long loops for non-coding work (outside benchmark tasks, no good long-loop reports for research or data analysis) and no quantitative rate of unattended runs failing or needing rollback (record failed runs and rollbacks alongside successes); no study of fresh-challenger relays; no standard for spend kill-switches; how many check-fix rounds to run. Long-run maintenance and quality of agent-built artifacts are poorly audited (vendor showcases and subsidised runs do not establish months-later quality). No rigorous estimate of runaway retries as a share of spend (record retry counts, no-progress iterations and their cost share). No quantitative conflict-rate data, such as the share of parallel merges needing rework (measure it in the run log). No head-to-head coding-quality data for LLM-judged best-of-N against one attempt, and no measured real-defect versus false-positive yield for multi-lens review (hypotheses to test). No validated measure of comprehension debt, and no published cost-per-accepted-output or defect-rate comparison at the heavy-spend end; outcome assertions and public spend totals do not establish payoff, so compare against your own matched baseline. Little rigorous evidence for adversarial design review outside math and question-answering, and no 2026 matched-cost comparison of reasoning-effort settings against best-of-N on real non-math tasks. No rigorous recent LLM-versus-human cost-per-label comparison or end-to-end bulk-judging case study with real total costs; current provider batch limits, caching terms and discounts are checked, not inferred.
